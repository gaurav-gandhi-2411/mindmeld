Every relative has shown up, and the table still isn't big enough
The tiny dish nobody orders, and everybody fights over first
Booked solid every December, three weeks before anyone thinks to ask
Wakes up the moment the tandoor's smoke reaches the street
Never quite as small as aunty promised while serving
Treated like god at the door, before anyone asks their name
Announces what's for dinner before anyone's called to eat
Shut inside clay heat, and trusted to come out right
Sets up the same cart on the same corner every single evening
At a big celebration, the line for seconds never actually ends
Lit before the sun's even up, and the last thing turned off at night
The last thing to run out at any Indian table, no matter how full everyone is

The corner store where the owner knows your order before you say it
Bought heavy and whole before a big occasion, never in small bags
A hot lunchbox that finds the right desk by one o'clock without fail
Where a stranger stops being a stranger before they've even entered the house
Refuses to let anyone leave the table with an empty plate
Locked at night, and everyone still knows exactly where the good biscuits are kept
A recipe followed exactly the way it was followed forty years ago
Gets the first hot roti off the pan, always, no matter who's hungrier
Says wash your hands first, and never point the sole of your foot at the food
